# ShopKart Brasil — Delivery Reliability: Executive Summary

## Business Problem
Operations and Customer Support teams independently flagged two concerns in early 2018: rising delivery delays, and rising customer complaints. Analysis shows these are not two separate problems — they share a common underlying issue.

## Key Findings
1. The late-delivery rate rose from 3.87% (H1 2017) to 8.63% (H1 2018) — more than double.
2. The strongest relationship we observed was with the delivery-promise buffer (the safety margin between the promised delivery date and the actual delivery date). When this buffer dropped below 8 days — as it did around Nov 2017 and Feb–Mar 2018 — the late rate jumped into double digits.
3. Late orders received an average rating of 2.27/5, compared to 4.29/5 for on-time orders — roughly half — and this pattern held consistently across every state we examined.
4. As a result, the share of orders receiving a rating of 1–2 ("low-rating rate") rose from 11.13% to 14.54% over the same period.
5. Most of the delivery time (~75%) is spent in transportation after the seller hands off the order, not in seller processing — seller handling time stayed stable at ~3 days throughout.

## Business Impact
If this trend continues, it puts customer satisfaction and repeat purchases at risk. The business already has a low repeat-purchase rate (3.12%), and a worsening delivery experience could pressure it further.

## Recommendations
1. During high-demand periods (e.g. Black Friday, seasonal peaks), keep delivery promises conservative — maintain at least an 8–10 day buffer.
2. Plan carrier capacity ahead of forecasted demand spikes, particularly in high-volume months.
3. Revisit the delivery-promise process so it adjusts for demand spikes rather than staying fixed year-round.

## Expected Outcome
The target is to bring late deliveries back toward the 4–5% range — levels already achieved in calmer months — and, as delivery performance improves, bring the low-rating rate back toward 11–12%.

## Limitations
GMV in this analysis is a proxy based on order value and freight charges, not reported company revenue. Seller-level late-delivery rates show where delivery performance differs, but they do not by themselves prove that a specific seller or carrier caused the delay. The relationship between product weight and late delivery was also weak, so weight alone does not explain delivery performance.

## KPIs to Monitor
Late Delivery Rate, Average Delivery-Promise Buffer, Customer Review Score (on-time vs. late split), Low-Rating Rate (share of orders rated 1–2), Repeat Purchase Rate.

---
*Narrative: Tighter delivery promises → more late deliveries → poorer ratings → higher low-rating rate → retention risk.*
